## LinkedIn

A human body replaces about 330 billion cells every day.

That is hard to square with how a body seems, looking much the same from one day to the next. The figure comes from Sender and Milo, in "The distribution of cellular turnover in the human body" (Nature Medicine, 2021). The abstract gives it as (0.33 ± 0.02) × 10^12 cells per day, so about 330 billion is the central estimate.

The abstract also breaks this turnover down in two ways, and they answer different questions. By cell count, close to 90% are blood cells. By mass, the total is 80 ± 20 grams per day, dominated by blood cells and gut epithelial cells.

These claims rest on the abstract alone, because the full paper didn't open.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-020-01182-9
