# Sounds Fake, Is True: Episode 1

**Fact:** A human body replaces about 330 billion cells every day.
**Length:** 30s
**Cold-audience pattern:** none when first written. Shot 1 was then revised on the person's decision after the first scroll-test verdict (scrolled at second 3; read as a did-you-know body fact page; second 3 looked identical to second 2).

On-screen text is exact. Cuts are hard and land on the beat.

## Shots

**1. Hook (3s, 0:00-0:03)**
- On screen: `TODAY A HUMAN BODY REPLACES ABOUT 330 BILLION CELLS`
- Moves: the words slam in as a six-line stack: `TODAY A HUMAN BODY` / `REPLACES` / `ABOUT` / `330` / `BILLION` / `CELLS`. `ABOUT`, `330` and `BILLION` land together, last, at 1.5s, with `330 BILLION` biggest. ## Source

Source: Sender R. & Milo R., "The distribution of cellular turnover in the human body", Nature Medicine (Springer Nature), 2021. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-020-01182-9 (DOI 10.1038/s41591-020-01182-9)

Sentences relied on, from the abstract:

> "We found a total cellular mass turnover of 80 ± 20 grams per day, dominated by blood cells and gut epithelial cells. In terms of cell numbers, close to 90% of the (0.33 ± 0.02) × 10^12 cells per day turnover was blood cells."

## Notes on the numbers

- 330 billion is the paper's 0.33 × 10^12 written out. It is the central estimate; the paper gives ± 0.02 × 10^12, so the screen always says `ABOUT`.
- `A HUMAN BODY`, not `YOUR BODY`: the text I read gives one figure for the human body and does not say it holds for every viewer.
- "Replaces" is the plain-language word for the paper's "turnover".
- Read: the abstract, on the Nature page and on Europe PMC. Not read: the full paper, which did not open.
- Left out on purpose: per-second figures and the 86% blood-cell share. They appear in secondary coverage but not in the text I read.
